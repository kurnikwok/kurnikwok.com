Abstract

This paper evaluates Product-managing shaped intelligence established through the direct approach within the bounded AI Shaping knowledge and publication product. The evidence question is whether it carried recurring accepted-state, source, owner-selection, priority, dependency, sequencing, stopping, resumption, reconciliation, propagation and closure burden while human product ownership, stewardship and decision authority remained controlling.

The evidence is a purposive eight-case first-party corpus within a window ending with accepted knowledge v1.23 and a dated snapshot of seventeen accepted Product-managing cycles as at 26 July 2026. The count defines the evidence window only; it is not effect size, proof or a public quality score. Three anchor cases receive narrative priority: successful governed continuation, human correction of an unsuitable route or format, and deliberate stopping or prevented unauthorised propagation.

Section 10 is the controlling public evidence test. Section 11 centralises the full limitations and unsupported claims. The evidence remains retrospective, purposively sampled, unquantified and not independently validated.

Public contribution

  • Evaluates a bounded product-level instance established through the direct approach.
  • Uses a purposive eight-case first-party corpus and a dated seventeen-cycle evidence window.
  • Gives narrative priority to successful continuation, human correction and deliberate stopping.
  • Centralises limitations and unsupported claims.

Public boundary

The paper supports initial bounded evaluation only. It does not establish quantified burden reduction, measured productivity improvement, independent validation, autonomy, generic transferability, universal Product-management capability, complete Scrum practice, deployment assurance or general superiority of the direct-shaping approach.
